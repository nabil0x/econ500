# Exam Priority Matrix — 501 Graduate Microeconomics

> **Purpose:** Rank all ~80 questions by exam frequency × difficulty.  
> **Exam format:** 8 sets × 14 marks, choose 5. Limited revision time → prioritise S-tier first.  
> **Scoring:**
> - **Frequency (1–5):** How many source documents contain this question. 1 src=2, 2 src=3, 3 src=5, +centrality adjustment.
> - **Difficulty (1–5):** 1=definition recall, 2=single derivation, 3=derivation+diagram, 4=full essay, 5=multi-step+diagram+essay.
> - **Priority Tier:** S=must master cold, A=priority, B=secondary, C=skimmable.

## Quick Study Guide

### S-Tier: Must Memorise Cold (~24 questions)

These are the questions that appear across the most source documents AND cover the core architecture of the course. **Start here.** For each S-tier question, you should be able to:
- Write the answer from memory in 8–12 minutes
- Draw every required diagram from scratch (indifference curves, GUPF, EV/CS/CV, Edgeworth box, 8-panel transmission, E(P) excess demand, production set)
- Perform every Lagrangian derivation (UMP, EMP, cost min) without consulting notes
- Recite the key numerical results: EV=124.24, CS=103.98, CV=87.9 (CD welfare); P*=23, Q=21k, DWL=100k (tax); p*=40, π=1800 (Bertrand); DWL_tariff=437.5k, DWL_VER=9.3M

**Study strategy:** Master the S-tier as a single coherent story. Start with preference foundations (CT 1A), flow through UMP→EMP→duality (CT 1B), then welfare measurement (WF Q1–Q3), then Pareto conditions + Welfare Theorems (GE 6B.Q3 + WF 5B.Q5), then build to GUPF/bliss point (WF 5A.Q2), the Pareto necessary/sufficient debate (WF 5B.Q1), and the capstone synthesis (WF 5B.Q8). Practice the 8-panel transmission mechanism and the E(P) existence/stability diagram separately — these are visual and require muscle memory. For numerical questions (CD welfare, tax DWL, Bertrand, tariff), memorise the template, not the numbers — exam variants only change parameters.

### A-Tier: Priority (~20 questions)

These are either (a) high-frequency but lower-difficulty foundation questions, or (b) single-source but important topics. **Study these after S-tier is solid.** Many A-tier questions fill in the gaps between S-tier pillars — they connect proofs and derivations that S-tier questions assume.

**Study strategy:** Group A-tier into clusters around S-tier pillars. For example, after studying the Welfare Theorems (S), add the externality questions (WF 5B.Q4, GE 8.Q2) and game theory (GE 7.Q1, GE 7.Q4). After mastering CD demand (S), add the behavioural properties (CT 1B.Q4) and the existence proof (CT 1A.Q5). The 6-game-theory questions (GE 7.Q1–Q6) form a natural cluster — study them as a block. The 2×2×2 model questions (GE 6B.Q1, Q5, Q7) also cluster. For A-tier numericals (DP 3A.Q7, DP 3C.Q1, GE 8.Q2, GE 9.Q1, GE 7.Q1), practise the computational steps as recipes.

### B-Tier: Secondary (~21 questions)

Questions that appear in only one source document or are variants of S/A-tier material. **Study if time permits after S and A are complete.** Spend no more than 5 minutes per question here — read the answer structure once, understand the logic, but don't memorise details. The key B-tier questions worth noting: the quasilinear corner (CT 1B.Q7), Willig bounds (WF Q5), the Leontief sausage maker (DP 3A.Q6), and the lemons market (GE 8.Q3) — these are shorter but still likely to appear.

**Study strategy:** Skim the answer file for B-tier questions during the last 2 days of revision. Focus on understanding what each question tests, not memorising the full answer. If a B-tier question appears on the exam, you should be able to reconstruct the logic from S/A-tier knowledge. For example, the three-good Walras' Law (DP 3C.Q2 / GE 6B.Q8) is just mechanical algebra built on the S-tier concept.

### C-Tier: Skimmable (~7 questions)

These are either duplicate/repeat questions (same content as an S or A entry) or very narrow definitional questions. **Skip unless you have extra time.** If a C-tier question appears, you can answer it from general knowledge acquired while studying the higher-tier questions.

**Study strategy:** Read the question title, confirm you understand what concept it references, move on. Do not spend any dedicated revision time on C-tier questions.
