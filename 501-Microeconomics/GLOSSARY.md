## 2. Core Assumptions

### 2.1 Preference Axioms

| Assumption | Definition | Why Needed | Violation Example | Explain |
|-----------|-----------|------------|-------------------|---------|
<a id="term-completeness"></a>
| **Completeness** | $\forall x,y:\; x\succeq y \lor y\succeq x$ | Consumer can always choose; utility representation requires a total order | "Brother of" relation — neither is brother of the other | [→](./GLOSSARY-DETAILED.md#detail-term-completeness) |
<a id="term-transitivity"></a>
| **Transitivity** | $x\succeq y \land y\succeq z \Rightarrow x\succeq z$ | No preference cycles; money-pump argument | Condorcet paradox, just-noticeable differences | [→](./GLOSSARY-DETAILED.md#detail-term-transitivity) |
| **Reflexivity** | $x \sim x$ (implied by completeness + transitivity) | Technical regularity | Trivially satisfied | [→](./GLOSSARY-DETAILED.md#detail-term-reflexivity) |
<a id="term-rationality"></a>

### 2.2 Desirability Axioms

### 2.3 Convexity & Continuity

| Assumption | Definition | Why Needed | Explain |
|-----------|-----------|------------|---------|
| **Convex preferences** | $y\succeq x \land z\succeq x \Rightarrow \lambda y + (1-\lambda)z \succeq x$ | Consumer prefers mixing/diversification; guarantees interior solutions | [→](./GLOSSARY-DETAILED.md#detail-term-convex-preferences) |
| **UCS convex** | $\{x : u(x) \ge \bar u\}$ is a convex set | Equivalent to convex preferences | [→](./GLOSSARY-DETAILED.md#detail-term-ucs-convex) |
| **Quasiconcave utility** | $u(\lambda x + (1-\lambda)y) \ge \min\{u(x), u(y)\}$ | Equivalent to UCS convex ⇔ convex preferences (same condition) | [→](./GLOSSARY-DETAILED.md#detail-term-quasiconcave-utility) |
<a id="term-debreu"></a>
<a id="term-continuity"></a>

| **Strict convexity** | $y\succeq x \land z\succeq x \land y\neq z \Rightarrow \lambda y+(1-\lambda)z \succ x$ | Unique demand (no flat segments on ICs) | [→](./GLOSSARY-DETAILED.md#detail-term-strict-convexity) |

| **Producer Surplus (PS)** | Revenue minus (opportunity cost = area below price, above supply/MC); reflects producer gains from trade | [DP 3C.Q1](./answers-demand-production.md#q-compet-01) | [→](./GLOSSARY-DETAILED.md#detail-term-producer-surplus) |
<a id="term-total-surplus"></a>
| **Total Surplus (TS)** | $CS + PS$; maximized at competitive equilibrium; DWL = loss from TS max | [WF Q1](./answers-welfare.md#q-welfare-01)–[Q5](./answers-welfare.md#q-welfare-05) | [→](./GLOSSARY-DETAILED.md#detail-term-total-surplus) |
<a id="term-potential-compensation"></a>
| **Potential Compensation Principle** | A change is efficient if gainers **could** compensate losers (Hicks-Kaldor); weaker than Pareto | [WF 5B.Q7](./answers-welfare.md#q-pareto-07) | [→](./GLOSSARY-DETAILED.md#detail-term-potential-compensation) |

**Welfare orderings (normal goods):**
- Price decrease: $EV > CS > CV > 0$
- Price increase: $CV < CS < EV < 0$
- Quasilinear: $CV = EV = CS$
