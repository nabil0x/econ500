## What Is Microeconomics About?

Microeconomics studies how individuals and firms make decisions and how markets allocate resources. It answers: *Why do consumers buy what they buy? How do firms decide what to produce? How do prices get determined?*

---

## Key Definitions

| Term | Simple Explanation |
|------|--------------------|
| **Utility** | A measure of satisfaction or happiness from consuming goods |
| **Preference** | How a consumer ranks different bundles of goods |
| **Indifference curve** | A curve showing all bundles that give the same utility |
| **Budget constraint** | What you can afford given prices and income |
| **Walrasian demand** | The optimal bundle a consumer chooses to maximize utility given prices and wealth |
| **Hicksian demand** | The optimal bundle a consumer chooses to minimize expenditure given prices and utility level |
| **Elasticity** | How much quantity responds to a change in price or income |
| **Production function** | The relationship between inputs (labor, capital) and output |
| **Profit** | Revenue minus cost |
| **Pareto optimal** | No one can be made better off without making someone worse off |
| **General equilibrium** | When ALL markets clear simultaneously (not just one) |

---

## The Two Big Micro Questions

### 1. Consumer Theory — How do people decide what to buy?

```
Preferences → Budget → Optimal Choice (maximize utility)
```

Key tools: Utility functions, indifference curves, budget lines, Lagrange multipliers.

**Three big results:**
- Walrasian demand x(p,w) — what you buy at given prices and wealth
- Indirect utility v(p,w) — the maximum utility you can achieve
- Expenditure function e(p,u) — the minimum cost to reach a given utility

### 2. Producer Theory — How do firms decide what to produce?

```
Technology → Costs → Optimal Production (maximize profit)
```

Key tools: Production functions, isoquants, cost minimization, profit maximization.

---

## The Connection Chain

```
Consumer Preferences → Individual Demand → Market Demand
                                                      ↓
General Equilibrium ← Market Supply ← Firm Production
```

**Partial equilibrium** = looking at one market in isolation.
**General equilibrium** = looking at ALL markets together (goods, labor, capital).

## Why This Matters for Your Exam

Microeconomics is the **foundation of everything else**:

| Micro Concept | Used In |
|---------------|---------|
| Utility maximization | Welfare analysis (CV/EV) — Macro welfare |
| Production functions | Solow growth model — Macro |
| General equilibrium | Economy-wide analysis — Macro & Development |
| Pareto optimality | Social welfare — Development & Political Economy |
| Elasticities | Trade policy — Development & Political Economy |
| Market failures | Government intervention — all courses |

## The Math You Need

| Tool | What It Does |
|------|-------------|
| **Lagrange multipliers** | Solve constrained optimization (max utility given budget) |
| **Envelope theorem** | How optimal value changes with parameters |
| **Roy's identity** | x(p,w) = -∂v/∂p ÷ ∂v/∂w — get demand from indirect utility |
| **Shephard's lemma** | h(p,u) = ∂e/∂p — get compensated demand from expenditure |
| **Slutsky equation** | Decompose price change into substitution + income effects |
