# Consumer Theory & Preferences — Questions

## 1A — Preference-Based Approach (10 questions)

### Question 1
What is the preference-based approach of consumer's behavior? Explain and interpret the assumptions of the preference based approach.

### Question 2
Define indifference set. Examine that if a preference relation satisfies strong monotonicity, its indifference curves must be downward sloping.

### Question 3
What are the twin definitions of convexity? In what respect are they different from strong convexity? Examine graphically.

### Question 4
Show that: Convexity of preference ⇔ USC(x) is convex ⇔ u(.) is quasiconcave.

### Question 5
Prove that if a preference relation satisfies monotonicity and continuity, then there exists a utility function u(.) representing such preference relation.

### Question 6
Define utility function. Explain the desirability of consumer's preference by the axiom of monotonicity and strong monotonicity.

### Question 7
Show that: Strong Monotonicity ⇒ Monotonicity ⇒ LNS.

### Question 8
Examine the following properties of preference relations:
i) Homogeneity
ii) Homotheticity

### Question 9
Prove that quasi-concave utility U(X,Y) is identical to dMRS/dX < 0.

### Question 10
A person's preferences: U = X + log(Y). Does this person have convex indifference curves? Provide proof.

## 1B — Utility Maximization & Demand (8 questions)

### Question 1

i) Derive the Walrasian demand functions and examine their properties.
ii) How do you get the indirect utility functions? Explain their properties and verify Roy's identity.
iii) Solve the expenditure minimization problem and examine the characteristics of the compensated demand function.

### Question 2
Examine the properties of Walrasian demand x(p,w). When can the sufficient condition of the UMP be violated?

### Question 3
Examine:
i) Homogeneity
ii) Homotheticity

### Question 4

i) Write the utility function.
ii) Find Marshallian demand for Gin.
iii) Find Hicksian demand for Campari.
iv) Find the expenditure function.
