## S-Tier Study Strategy: Must Memorise Cold (~10 Questions)

These are the questions that appear across the most source documents AND cover the core architecture of macroeconomics. **Start here — these are non-negotiable.** For each S-tier question, you should be able to write the full answer from memory in under 10 minutes.

### Solow Block (Q1–3) — Days 1–2

**Practice drill (repeat 3× without notes):**
1. Write the six neoclassical features (CRS, diminishing MP, Inada, perfect competition, exogenous s, exogenous g/n).
2. Derive k̇: start from K̇ = sY − δK → ln k = ln K − ln A − ln L → differentiate → substitute → boxed equation.
3. Draw the Solow phase diagram: sf(k) below (n+g+δ)k to the left of k\*, crossing at k\*. Label both axes, the arrow direction on the k-axis, and steady-state k\*.
4. State BGP growth rates: k,y,c constant per effective worker; K,Y,C per worker grow at g; aggregate at n+g.
5. For Cobb-Douglas: derive k\* = (s/(n+g+δ))^{1/(1−α)}, y\* = k\*^{α}, c\* = (1−s)k\*^{α}.
6. Derive Golden Rule: c\*(s) = (1−s)(s/(n+g+δ))^{α/(1−α)} → differentiate → s_gold = α. Show hump shape.
7. Welfare analysis: starting from k_gold, raising s → c drops on impact → new steady state c\*\* < c\* → permanent welfare loss. Envelope theorem logic.

**Key numbers to memorise:** α = 1/3 (capital share), λ = (1−α)(n+g+δ) ≈ 0.053 (convergence speed), half-life ≈ 13 years, s_gold = α = 1/3.

### Ramsey Block (Q4–7) — Days 2–3

**Practice drill (repeat 3× without notes):**
1. Write the utility function U = ∫ e^{−ρt} (c^{1−θ}−1)/(1−θ) · L/H dt and constraint k̇ = f(k) − c − (n+g+δ)k.
2. Construct the Hamiltonian: H = e^{−(ρ−n)t} u(c) + λ[f(k) − c − (n+g+δ)k].
3. Derive FOCs: ∂H/∂c = 0 → c^{−θ}e^{−(ρ−n)t} = λ; ∂H/∂k = −λ̇ → λ[f'(k) − (n+g+δ)] = −λ̇.
4. Eliminate λ: take logs of FOC1 → differentiate → substitute FOC2 → ċ/c = (1/θ)(f'(k) − ρ − θg − δ).
5. Draw the phase diagram: vertical ċ=0 at k\* (where f'(k\*) = ρ+θg+δ), hump-shaped k̇=0 peaking at k_gold, 4 directional quadrants (NW: k↓ c↑, NE: k↓ c↓, SW: k↑ c↑, SE: k↑ c↓), saddle path (unique convergent trajectory).
6. Derive Modified Golden Rule: f'(k\*) = ρ+θg+δ. Show k\* < k_gold when ρ > 0. State that k\* depends on preferences (ρ,θ) while k_gold is purely technological.
7. Define competitive equilibrium: household optimization + firm optimization + market clearing + TVC.

### Endogenous Growth Block (Q8–10) — Days 4–5

**Practice drill (repeat 3× without notes):**
1. Write R&D equation: Ā = B(a^L L)^γ A^φ.
2. Derive g_A: g_A = Ā/A = B(a^L L)^γ A^{φ−1}.
3. Differentiate: ġ_A/g_A = γn + (φ−1)g_A (key dynamic equation).
4. Set ġ_A = 0: g_A\* = γn/(1−φ). Note this is independent of a^L — a^L has level effect only.
5. For the (g_A, g_K) system: ġ_A=0 locus is βg_K = (1−φ)g_A − θn; ġ_K=0 locus is g_K = g_A + n.
6. Draw the (g_A, g_K) phase diagram with both loci. Show the BGP intersection and how n↑ shifts ġ_A=0 down and ġ_K=0 up (higher g_A\*).
7. State the three features of knowledge: non-rivalry (use by many), partial excludability (patents), cumulativeness (standing on shoulders). Reference Romer (1990).

### Study Strategy for S-Tier

Master the S-tier as a single coherent story: **Solow** (exogenous growth engine, diminishing returns, k̇ equation, phase diagram, Golden Rule) → **Ramsey** (endogenous saving — households choose consumption, Euler equation, phase diagram, Modified GR, k\* < k_gold) → **Endogenous Growth** (why knowledge is special — non-rival, increasing returns, R&D equation, level vs growth effects, Jones critique). Each model builds on and improves the previous one. Practice every derivation at least 3 times without notes. Draw every phase diagram until you can reproduce it blindfolded. The S-tier questions cover roughly 60% of potential exam marks.

## A-Tier Study Strategy: Priority (~14 Questions)

These are (a) the concepts the task specifically flags as A-tier (comparative statics, speed of convergence, endogenous vs semi-endogenous), (b) cross-source unanswered topics (OLG, RBC, NK, R&D Monopolistic), and (c) single-source but important policy applications.

### Speed of Convergence Cluster (Q12–13) — Day 5

**Solow linearization (simpler — master first):**
- Taylor expand k̇ = sf(k) − (n+g+δ)k around k\*.
- Use sf'(k\*) = α(n+g+δ) from steady state condition.
- Get k̇ ≈ −(1−α)(n+g+δ)(k−k\*) → λ = (1−α)(n+g+δ).
- With α=1/3, n+g+δ=0.08: λ=0.053, half-life=13 years.

**Ramsey linearization (harder — full 2×2 system):**
- Jacobian matrix: J = [[f'(k\*)−(n+g+δ), −1], [c\*f''(k\*)/θ, 0]].
- Trace = ρ−n+(θ−1)g, determinant = −c\*f''(k\*)/θ > 0.
- One negative, one positive eigenvalue → saddle-path stability.
- φ_ramsey ≈ 0.04 → half-life ≈ 17 years (slower than Solow due to consumption smoothing).

### Convergence and Empirics Cluster (Q14 + Q19) — Day 6

- Know Barro (1991) and MRW (1992) results: conditional convergence at 2-3% per year.
- Understand why unconditional convergence fails (different steady states across countries).
- MRW augmented Solow with human capital → explains ~80% of cross-country income variation.
- Growth accounting: Ẏ/Y = Ȧ/A + αK̇/K + (1−α)L̇/L. Solow residual = TFP growth.
- Convergence clubs (Durlauf & Johnson, 1995): countries cluster into groups converging to different steady states. Twin-peaked distribution (Quah, 1996).
- Poverty traps: S-shaped s(k)f(k) creates 3 intersections with break-even line → 2 stable (low, high) + 1 unstable (middle threshold).
- Big Push (Sachs, 2005): coordinated investment across multiple sectors to escape poverty trap.

### Comparative Statics and Policy (Q15–18) — Day 6–7

- Practice all 4 Solow shocks on diagram: δ↓ (break-even rotates down → k\*↑), g↑ (break-even rotates up → k\*↓), α↑ (sf(k) shape changes → ambiguous), s↑ (sf(k) shifts up → k\*↑).
- Level effects vs growth effects proof: ln y(t) = ln A(0) + gt + [α/(1−α)][ln s − ln(n+g+δ)]. ∂ln y(t)/∂t = g independent of s.
- Government spending in Ramsey: key insight — ċ=0 locus does NOT shift (depends only on f'(k), not G). Only k̇=0 shifts down by G. Permanent shock → c jumps down immediately.
- ρ and θ effects: higher ρ → more impatience → lower k\*. Higher θ → lower IES → flatter consumption growth response.
- Romer L_R exercise: 10% increase in L_R → g_A jumps by β×10% on impact → returns to g_A\* = βn/(1−θ) in long run. Only level effect on A, no long-run growth effect.

### Unanswered Topics: Write Answers First (Q20–25) — Days 7–8

These are high-priority because they appear in BOTH Batch 47 and 48. Write answers using the batch files as source material:

- **OLG (Q20–21):** Two-period model, discrete time. Individual maximizes U = u(c_1) + (1+ρ)^{−1}u(c_2) subject to c_1 + s = w and c_2 = (1+r)s. Derive Euler: u'(c_1) = (1+r)/(1+ρ) · u'(c_2). For log utility: s = w/(2+ρ). Derive k_{t+1} from saving = capital next period. Compare convergence speed with Solow.
- **R&D Monopolistic (Q22):** Y = [∫ L(i)^φ di]^{1/φ} with 0<φ<1. Demand for patent: L(i) = Y·(p(i)/P)^{−1/(1−φ)}. Profit = p(i)L(i) − wL(i). PV of profits = ∫_t^∞ π e^{−R(s)} ds. Equilibrium L_A: v·Ȧ = wL_A where v = PV of patent.
- **RBC (Q23):** Household max E_0 Σ β^t[ln c_t + v(1−l_t)]. Short-run labor supply: l_s = l(w, r, ...) from intra-temporal FOC: v'(1−l)/u'(c) = w. Long-run independence: when ρ = φ (discount rate = persistence), labor supply independent of wage.
- **NK (Q24–25):** Lucas supply: y = y_n + α(p − p^e) + ε. NK IS: ċ/c = σ(r_t − ρ) → x_t = E_t[x_{t+1}] − (1/σ)(i_t − E_tπ_{t+1} − r^n_t). NK PC: π_t = βE_tπ_{t+1} + κx_t (divine coincidence: stabilizing inflation also stabilizes output gap). Taylor rule: i_t = r^n_t + φ_ππ_t + φ_xx_t.

## B-Tier Study: Secondary (~5 Questions)

Questions that appear in only one source or are variants of S/A-tier material. **Study if time permits after S and A are complete.**

**Details per question:**
- **Q26 (Types of tech progress):** Understand three forms — Hicks-neutral (Y = A·F(K,L)), Harrod-neutral/labor-augmenting (Y = F(K, AL) — used in Solow), Solow-neutral/capital-augmenting (Y = F(AK, L)). Uzawa's theorem: sustained growth requires labor-augmenting technical change. For Cobb-Douglas, all three are equivalent. Easy 2-mark question — definition recall.
- **Q27 (Bangladesh demographic transition):** Fertility fell from 6.6 to 2.2 (1975–2023). In Solow: n↓ → break-even line rotates down → k\*↑. The demographic dividend accounts for ~1/3 of East Asian growth (Bloom, Canning & Sevilla, 2003).
- **Q28 (Taxation):** Lump-sum tax: no distortion, Ricardian equivalence. Capital income tax τ_K: ċ/c = ((1−τ_K)f'(k) − δ − ρ − θg)/θ → ċ=0 shifts left → k\*↓. Chamley-Judd: optimal long-run τ_K = 0. Labor income tax with inelastic labor = lump-sum.
- **Q29 (R&D vs Solow):** Memorise the 5-dimension comparison table: technology (exogenous vs endogenous), returns (CRS vs IRS), market structure (perfect vs monopolistic competition), long-run driver (population vs innovation), policy (level effects vs possible growth effects).
- **Q30 (Hansen indivisible labor):** Lotteries over employment states → agents either work full-time (h=1) or not at all (h=0). This makes aggregate labor supply more elastic than individual micro elasticities. Hansen calibration: labor supply elasticity ≈ ∞ (horizontal) vs micro estimate ≈ 0.2. Resolves the RBC labor-supply puzzle.

**Study strategy:** Skim the answer files for B-tier questions. Spend no more than 5 minutes per question. Focus on understanding what each question tests, not memorising the full answer. If a B-tier question appears, reconstruct the logic from S/A-tier knowledge.

## C-Tier: Skimmable (~2 Questions)

- **Q31 (R&D social vs private L_A):** This is a sub-part of the R&D Monopolistic question (Q22). The wedge exists because private returns < social returns (knowledge spillovers not fully appropriable). Socially optimal L_A > equilibrium L_A. Standard Pigouvian subsidy logic.
- **Q32 (Elasticity numerical):** Formula ε_{y\*,n} = −(α/(1−α))·(n/(n+g+δ)). With α=1/3, n falls from 2% to 1%: log approximation gives Δln y\* ≈ 0.078 → y\* rises ~7.8%. Know the formula and the numerical result. This is a mechanical computation — very likely to appear as a sub-part of a larger question.

**Study strategy:** Read the question title, confirm you understand the concept, move on. Do not spend any dedicated revision time on C-tier questions.
