## What Is Macroeconomics About?

Macroeconomics studies the economy as a WHOLE — GDP growth, inflation, unemployment, and why some countries grow rich while others stay poor. It answers: *Why do economies grow? What causes recessions? How do policy choices affect everyone?*

---

## Key Definitions

| Term | Simple Explanation |
|------|--------------------|
| **GDP** | Total value of everything produced in a country in a year |
| **Economic growth** | Increase in GDP per person over time |
| **Steady state** | When capital per worker stops changing — the economy is in balance |
| **Capital deepening** | Giving each worker more machines/tools to work with |
| **Technological progress** | Better ideas, methods, and technology that make workers more productive |
| **Depreciation** | The wear and tear of capital — machines break down over time |
| **Golden Rule** | The level of capital that maximizes long-run consumption |
| **Conditional convergence** | Poor countries grow faster than rich ones *if* they have similar institutions, policies, etc. |
| **Intertemporal choice** | How people decide between consuming today vs saving for tomorrow |

## The Central Question: Why Do Countries Grow?

### The Production Function

```
Y = F(K, AL)
Output = f(Capital, Effective Labor)
```

**Three levers for growth:**
1. **More capital** (K↑) — diminishing returns (each machine helps less than the last)
2. **More labor** (L↑) — population growth
3. **Better technology** (A↑) — no diminishing returns, the only sustainable source of growth

---

## The Two Growth Models in Your Exam

### Solow Model (Simpler)

```
Investment = s·Y
Break-even = (n + g + δ)·k

k ↑ when investment > break-even
k ↓ when investment < break-even
k stays same (steady state) when investment = break-even
```

**What it teaches:** Capital accumulation drives growth in the short run, but ONLY technology drives growth in the long run.

### Ramsey-Cass-Koopmans Model (More Advanced)

Same as Solow, but **households decide how much to save** (instead of a fixed saving rate). They choose consumption over time to maximize total lifetime happiness.

Adds: Phase diagram, saddle-path stability, speed of adjustment.

---

## The Big Trade-off

```
      Consume today (happiness now)
                 VS
      Save/invest (more growth, happiness later)
```

| Model | Who Decides Saving? | How Fast Convergence? |
|-------|--------------------|----------------------|
| Solow | Fixed rate (s) | Faster |
| Ramsey | Optimizing households | Slower (consumption smoothing) |

## The Math You Need

| Tool | Where It Appears |
|------|-----------------|
| **Cobb-Douglas production** | Y = K^α (AL)^(1-α) — main production function |
| **Per-worker transformation** | y = k^α (dividing by effective labor) |
| **Steady state algebra** | Set k̇ = 0, solve for k* |
| **Phase diagrams** | k̇=0 and ċ=0 loci — separate graphs |
| **Taylor approximation** | Speed of convergence λ = (1-α)(n+g+δ) |
