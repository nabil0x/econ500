## What Is This Topic About?

How we measure who's working, who's not, and what kind of work people do. These are the fundamental statistics for understanding Bangladesh's economy.

---

## Key Definitions — The Employment Status Wheel

```
              Total Population
                    │
         Working-Age (15+)        │    Below 15
                    │
        ┌───────────┴───────────┐
    Labour Force          Not in Labour Force
   (working or seeking)   (students, retired,
        │                  homemakers, etc.)
    ┌───┴───┐
Employed  Unemployed
 (worked  (no work,
  ≥1 hr)   seeking)
```

| Term | Simple Explanation | Bangladesh (2022) |
|------|--------------------|-------------------|
| **Working-age** | People aged 15+ | 119.4M |
| **Labour force** | People working OR actively looking for work | 73M |
| **Employed** | Worked at least 1 hour for pay/profit | 70.5M |
| **Unemployed** | No work, available, actively seeking | 2.6M |
| **Underemployed** | Working <35 hrs/week but want more hours | 2.8M |
| **Not in labour force** | Not working and not looking (students, homemakers, retired) | 46.3M |

---

## Key Rates (Formulas)

| Rate | Formula | Bangladesh |
|------|---------|-----------|
| **LFPR** | Labour Force ÷ Working-Age Population | 61.2% |
| **Unemployment Rate** | Unemployed ÷ Labour Force | 3.53% |

---

## Why 1 Hour? — The "At Least 1 Hour" Rule

The international standard counts someone as "employed" if they worked **at least 1 hour** in the reference week. This means:
- Many "employed" people are actually underemployed
- The unemployment rate (3.53%) looks low, but many workers are in poor-quality jobs
- **Always look beyond the unemployment rate** for developing countries

- **These are your numbers** for any question about Bangladesh's economy

- **Gender gap** — female LFPR (42.8%) is half of male (80%) — connects to Kabeer
- **Informality** — over 50% are own-account workers (no formal employer)
