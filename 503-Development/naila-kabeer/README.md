## 1. The Bangladesh Paradox

**Core idea:** Bangladesh achieved major social progress despite extreme poverty, oppressive patriarchy, and weak governance.

| Dimension | Expected | Actual |
|-----------|----------|--------|
| **Economic** | Progress needs wealth first | Outperforms richer countries on social indicators |
| **Political** | Good governance required | Corrupt state still delivered pro-poor outcomes |
| **Cultural** | Islam incompatible with women's rights | Gender equality improved alongside rising conservatism |

**Kabeer's thesis:** The paradox is explained by **ordinary women renegotiating patriarchy** — not just by state/NGO action.

## 2. Patriarchy in Bangladesh

### "Classic Patriarchy" (Kandiyoti 1988, Cain 1984)

Bangladesh sits in the belt from North Africa → Middle East → Northern South Asia.

**Features:**
| Feature | Meaning |
|---------|---------|
| Patrilineal | Inheritance through father |
| Patrilocal | Wives move to husband's family |
| Patriarchal | Men hold power |
| Endogamous | Marriage within same group |

**Consequences:**
- "Missing women" (Sen 1990) — >100 million globally
- Sex ratios: 106–110 men per 100 women
- Strong son preference → high fertility
- Dowry, early marriage, restricted mobility

### North vs South South Asia (Dyson & Moore 1983)

| Feature | North (Bangladesh) | South |
|---------|-------------------|-------|
| Women's mobility | Restricted | Greater freedom |
| Fertility | Higher | Lower |
| Sex ratios | 106–114 M:100F | 97–107 M:100F |

## 3. Key Concepts

### Agency
- *"Capacity to define goals and act upon them within social constraints"* (Giddens 1979)
- **Spectrum:** adaptive preferences → hidden transcripts → bargaining → overt resistance

### Resources (Bourdieu)
| Type | Examples |
|------|----------|
| Material | Land, livestock, finance, technology |
| Human | Skills, education, health |
| Social | Networks, relationships, obligations |
| Symbolic | Status, prestige, authority |

### Patriarchal Bargains (Kandiyoti 1988)
- Women pursue security **within** patriarchy, not against it
- Producing sons = status, protection, old-age security

### Household Bargaining
- Economic contribution → stronger voice in fertility, food, education decisions
- Mothers less likely to discriminate against daughters

---

## 4. Employment & Empowerment Pathways

**Two pathways** (Sen 1990b):
1. **Societal:** Visible work → society values women more → fairer resource share
2. **Household:** Paid work → financial contribution → bargaining power

**Evidence:** Districts with higher female economic activity + literacy → lower fertility, lower female disadvantage in child survival (Drèze & Sen 1995)

## 5. Indicators of Change — Bangladesh vs India vs Pakistan

| Indicator | Bangladesh | India | Pakistan |
|-----------|-----------|-------|----------|
| Fertility rate | **2.2** | 2.6 | 3.4 |
| Female life expectancy | **70 yrs** | 67 yrs | 66 yrs |
| Infant mortality (per 1000) | **37** | 47 | 59 |
| Sex ratio at birth | **105:100 (normal)** | 111:100 (abnormal) | Some rise |

**Key findings:**
- Strong decline in son preference
- Most families now prefer balanced sex composition
- People saw themselves as active participants

## 6. How Change Happens

> *"Change within patriarchy occurs not through rupture, but through cumulative acts of renegotiation."*

- Social instability → gaps between internalised norms and reality
- Life-course disruptions
- New resources through government, markets, civil society
- Incremental adjustments + everyday negotiations
