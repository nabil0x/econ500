## 1. Context — Why Trade Structure Matters for Development

A country's trade structure is a **key determinant of its development trajectory**. The relationship between trade and development has been debated since the classical economists:

| School of Thought | View on Trade | Relevance to Bangladesh |
|-------------------|---------------|------------------------|
| **Classical (Smith, Ricardo)** | Trade as engine of growth — comparative advantage benefits all | Bangladesh's RMG-led growth follows this logic |
| **Dependency (Frank, Prebisch)** | Trade reproduces centre-periphery inequality — declining terms of trade | Bangladesh's RMG concentration and low value addition echo this concern |
| **Neoclassical (Bhagwati, Krueger)** | Openness drives efficiency — protectionism hurts growth | Bangladesh's high tariffs contradict this prescription |
| **Washington Consensus** | Trade liberalisation as core reform | Bangladesh's partial liberalisation is a contested policy choice |

Bangladesh's external trade challenges matter because they are **not temporary problems** — they are structural features inherited from the country's growth model. Addressing them is essential for sustaining growth beyond LDC graduation.

## 2. Challenge 1: Declining Trade Orientation

| Metric | 2012 | 2022 | Trend |
|--------|------|------|-------|
| Trade-to-GDP ratio | 48.1% | **33.8%** | Declining |
| Exports-to-GDP | ~20% | ~13% | Declining |
| Imports-to-GDP | ~28% | ~20% | Declining |

### Comparison with Peers

| Country | Trade-to-GDP (2022) |
|---------|---------------------|
| Bangladesh | **33.8%** |
| Vietnam | >150% |
| India | ~45% |
| Sri Lanka | ~45% |
| Pakistan | ~30% |

**Significance:** A declining trade-to-GDP ratio means Bangladesh is becoming **less integrated** into the global economy at a time when other developing countries are deepening their integration. This signals:

- Erosion of international competitiveness.
- Insufficient participation in global value chains (GVCs).
- Declining export dynamism — the RMG sector's growth is not pulling up the rest of the economy.

**What it means:** Bangladesh's growth is increasingly **domestically driven** (consumption ~70% of GDP). While this reduces exposure to global shocks in the short run, it limits technology transfer, productivity growth, and long-run catch-up.

## 3. Challenge 2: Extreme Export Concentration (RMG Dominance)

| Indicator | 2010s | 2023 |
|-----------|-------|------|
| RMG share of total exports | ~77% | **86%** |
| RMG export value | ~$20B | ~$47B |
| Export concentration index | Moderate | >2× India, Indonesia, Vietnam |

### The Risk

Bangladesh's export concentration in ready-made garments is one of the **highest in the world**:

- Oil exporters (Venezuela, Nigeria) typically dominate the list — Bangladesh is the only manufacturing exporter with this level of concentration.
- The sector faces simultaneous pressures: LDC graduation (tariff preference loss), technological change (automation, 3D knitting, man-made fibre shift), buyer consolidation, and rising competition from Ethiopia, Cambodia, and Vietnam.

### Non-RMG Export Performance

| Sector | Export Value (2023) | Trend |
|--------|-------------------|-------|
| Leather & leather goods | ~$1.2B | Modest growth |
| Jute & jute goods | ~$1.0B | Declining |
| Pharmaceuticals | ~$150M | Growing (but small base) |
| IT/ITES | ~$1.5B | Growing |
| Frozen food (shrimp) | ~$0.5B | Stagnant |

**If RMG faces a shock**, no other sector is large enough to buffer the impact. This is the single greatest vulnerability in Bangladesh's development model.

## 4. Challenge 3: Capital Flight through Trade Mis-Invoicing

| Period | Annual Average Loss | Method |
|--------|-------------------|--------|
| 2009–2018 | **~$8.27 billion** | Over-invoicing imports + under-invoicing exports |
| FY2022–23 | Financial account outflows of 0.5% of GDP | IMF reported |

### How Trade Mis-Invoicing Works

```
Over-invoicing imports:
Firm imports $100 worth of goods, declares $150 → $50 illegally transferred abroad

Under-invoicing exports:
Firm exports $100 worth of goods, declares $70 → difference held abroad
```

### Why It Matters

- **Drains foreign exchange reserves** — at a time when reserves have fallen from $48 billion (2021) to ~$20 billion (2024).
- **Indicates governance failure** — customs and banking oversight are weak.
- **Reduces taxable trade** — the government loses tariff and VAT revenue.
- **Distorts trade data** — official trade statistics become unreliable.

## 5. Challenge 4: Export Data Mismatch (EPB vs Bangladesh Bank)

| Fiscal Year | EPB Reported | Bangladesh Bank | Gap |
|-------------|-------------|----------------|-----|
| 2020–21 | — | — | **$4.73 billion** |
| 2022–23 | — | — | **$12 billion** |
| 2023–24 (Jul–Apr) | $47.47 billion | $33.67 billion | **$13.8 billion** |

### Causes of the Gap

| Cause | Explanation |
|-------|-------------|
| **Different reporting methods** | EPB uses FOB-based customs data; BB uses actual forex receipts |
| **Time lags** | Exports shipped in one period may be paid in another |
| **Repatriation delays** | Export proceeds not brought back within the mandated timeframe |
| **Under-invoicing** | Some of the gap reflects capital flight |

### Why It Matters

A sustained gap of this magnitude — approaching $14 billion — undermines:

- **Investor confidence** — which data set is reliable?
- **Policy planning** — the government cannot accurately track export performance.
- **External credibility** — international ratings agencies and donors flag data inconsistencies.

## 6. Challenge 5: RMG Sector Unsustainability

| Issue | Detail | Risk Level |
|-------|--------|------------|
| **Declining value addition** | Raw material import costs rose to 83% of export value (FY22) | High |
| **Cotton dependency** | Bangladesh relies on imported cotton; global trend is shifting to man-made fibre (MMF) | Critical |
| **LDC graduation (2026)** | Loss of EU GSP preferences → RMG exports face 9–12% tariffs | Critical |
| **Buyer concentration** | Top 10 buyers account for ~60% of RMG exports | High |
| **Automation risk** | 3D knitting and automated sewing reduce labour cost advantage | Medium-term |
| **Labour compliance** | Rana Plaza legacy; ILO standards; reputational risk | Ongoing |

### The Value Addition Problem

```
RMG Export Value: 100
  - Imported raw materials (fabric, yarn): 83
  = Domestic value addition: 17
```

Bangladesh adds only ~17% of the export value domestically — the rest is imported inputs. This means the domestic economic benefit of RMG exports is far smaller than the gross export figure suggests.

## 7. Challenge 6: High Tariff Regime and Anti-Export Bias

| Entity | Average Tariff |
|--------|---------------|
| Bangladesh | **27%** |
| Low-income countries average | 9.79% |
| Lower-middle-income average | ~12% |
| Global average | **6%** |
| Vietnam | ~6% |

### How Anti-Export Bias Works

```
High tariffs on imported inputs
        ↓
Exporters pay inflated prices for machinery, fabric, chemicals
        ↓
Production costs are higher than competitors
        ↓
Selling domestically (protected market) is more profitable than exporting
        ↓
Firms choose domestic market over exports
        ↓
Export diversification is discouraged
```

### Sectoral Tariff Rates

| Sector | Tariff Rate | Impact |
|--------|------------|--------|
| Textiles (backward linkage) | High | Increases RMG input costs |
| Capital machinery | Moderate | Discourages industrial upgrading |
| Consumer goods | Very high (>50% on some items) | Protects inefficient domestic producers |
| Intermediate goods | High | Raises costs for all manufacturing |

## 8. Challenge 7: Limited FTA Participation

| Trade Agreement | Bangladesh Status | Comparator (Vietnam) |
|----------------|-------------------|---------------------|
| **Bhutan FTA** | ✅ Signed (2020) — narrow scope | — |
| **SAFTA** | ✅ Member | — |
| **BIMSTEC FTA** | ❌ Delayed | — |
| **RCEP** | ❌ Not joined | ✅ Member |
| **CPTPP** | ❌ Not joined | ✅ Member |
| **EU FTA** | ❌ Not applicable (GSP only) | ✅ EU-Vietnam FTA |
| **Bilateral with China** | ❌ Under discussion | — |

### Consequences

- Bangladesh's exporters face **higher tariffs** than competitors who have FTAs.
- Bangladesh cannot use FTAs to **diversify export markets**.
- The country has **limited bargaining power** in global trade negotiations.
- Post-LDC graduation, the lack of FTAs will be more costly as GSP preferences are lost.

## 10. Policy Recommendations

| Challenge | Recommended Action | Timeline |
|-----------|-------------------|----------|
| **Trade orientation** | Target trade-to-GDP >50% by 2030 through export promotion and import facilitation | Medium-term |
| **Export concentration** | Diversify into MMF textiles, electronics assembly, pharmaceuticals, IT services | Long-term |
| **Capital flight** | Digital customs tracking (ASYCUDA); cross-verification EPB-BB data; strengthen tax administration | Short-term |
| **Data mismatch** | Single integrated trade data system; harmonise EPB and BB reporting methods | Short-term |
| **RMG sustainability** | Shift to MMF; green manufacturing (200+ LEED factories); backward linkage development | Medium-term |
| **Tariff regime** | Strategic liberalisation of intermediate and capital goods inputs | Medium-term |
| **FTA participation** | Join RCEP; expedite BIMSTEC; negotiate strategic bilateral FTAs with China, Japan, Korea | Medium-term |

## 11. Exam Tips

### Structure for the 15-Mark Answer

- **Organise around the "seven concerns"** — each challenge is ~2 marks (2 × 7 = 14) + introduction/conclusion.
- **Use the comparison table** for each challenge — Bangladesh vs Vietnam, regional peers, or global averages.
- **Link to theory** — mention the trade-development debate (comparative advantage vs dependency).
- **Connect to Political Economy** — especially RMG sector notes (value addition) and Washington Consensus (trade liberalisation).
- **End with policy recommendations** — even a brief version shows applied thinking.

### Key Data Points to Memorise

| Data Point | Value |
|------------|-------|
| Trade-to-GDP | 33.8% (2022) |
| RMG share of exports | 86% |
| Average tariff | 27% |
| Capital flight (annual) | ~$8.27 billion |
| EPB-BB data gap (2023–24) | $13.8 billion |
| RMG domestic value addition | ~17% |
| Number of FTAs | 1 (Bhutan) |

### Common Mistakes

- Presenting trade challenges as a list without **connecting them** (they are interrelated — tariffs discourage diversification, which concentrates exports, which makes data mismatch more costly, etc.).
- Forgetting the **RMG value addition** problem (gross export figures overstate the domestic benefit).
- Not linking to **LDC graduation 2026** — this is the most urgent time pressure.
- Presenting policy recommendations without prioritisation.
- Ignoring the positive side — RMG has been a tremendous success; the challenges are about what comes next.

## 12. Professor's Corner

> The external trade questions are Bangladesh's version of the **export concentration problem** that appears in every development economics textbook. Venezuela has oil, Nigeria has oil, Zambia has copper — and Bangladesh has RMG. The commodity is different, but the structural vulnerability is the same.
>
> **The deeper analytical question:**
>
> Is Bangladesh's RMG concentration a **developmental success** (export-led growth that lifted millions from poverty) or a **dependency trap** (a single sector that blocks structural transformation)? The answer is both — and reconciling these two perspectives is the core analytical challenge.
>
> **What makes Bangladesh's case distinctive:**
>
> 1. RMG is a **manufactured good**, not a primary commodity. This breaks the usual resource-curse pattern — Bangladesh is not a mono-commodity exporter in the traditional sense. Yet the concentration is similar.
>
> 2. The RMG sector has had **genuine developmental effects** — 4 million jobs (60% women), forward and backward linkages, infrastructure development, and tax revenue. Unlike oil in Venezuela, RMG employs large numbers of people directly.
>
> 3. However, the **domestic value addition** is low (~17%), meaning a large share of the economic benefit leaks out as payments for imported inputs. The gross export figure dramatically overstates the domestic benefit.
>
> **Reconciling the two views:**
>
> The answer is to see RMG as a **platform** — an entry point into global manufacturing that has succeeded in its primary purpose (employment, foreign exchange, poverty reduction) but must now either **deepen** (increase domestic value addition) or **widen** (diversify into other sectors). The seven challenges are essentially obstacles to deepening and widening.
