## What Is This Topic About?

This topic explains why Bangladesh — a poor, deeply patriarchal country — achieved remarkable progress in gender equality and women's wellbeing, and how ordinary women drove this change.

---

## Key Definitions

| Term | Simple Explanation |
|------|--------------------|
| **Patriarchy** | A social system where men hold primary power and women are subordinate |
| **Agency** | The ability to make choices and act on them |
| **Resources** | What you have to work with (money, skills, relationships, status) |
| **Achievements** | What you're able to do or become as a result |
| **Patriarchal bargain** | Strategies women use to get the best deal possible within a patriarchal system (e.g., having sons for security) |
| **Habitus** | Deeply ingrained habits, beliefs, and behaviors you learn from your environment |
| **Missing women** | The gap between how many women *should* exist vs actually do — caused by discrimination |
| **Son preference** | Cultural preference for male children over female |

---

## The Big Picture — "The Bangladesh Paradox"

| What You'd Expect | What Actually Happened |
|-------------------|----------------------|
| Poor country = poor social outcomes | Bangladesh outperformed richer countries |
| Strong patriarchy = no women's progress | Gender equality improved significantly |
| Weak governance = no development | Pro-poor outcomes despite corruption |
| Rising Islamic conservatism = women lose rights | Women's agency actually increased |

---

## The Three Key Actors in the Story

| Actor | Role |
|-------|------|
| **Women themselves** | Main drivers — renegotiated patriarchy through everyday actions |
| **State & NGOs** | Created opportunities (education, microfinance, health programs) |
| **Global forces** | Industrialization, RMG sector, women's paid employment |

> **Key insight:** Change happened NOT through revolution, but through millions of small acts of renegotiation.
