## Key Concepts

| Concept | Definition |
|---------|------------|
| **Washington Consensus** | Neoliberal policy package (fiscal discipline, trade liberalization, privatization) imposed via WB/IMF conditionality |
| **Modernization Theory** | Post-WWII theory that development is a linear path from traditional to modern societies; critiqued for Eurocentrism |
| **Dependency Theory** | Core nations systematically underdevelop the periphery through unequal exchange and surplus extraction |
| **World Systems Theory** | Wallerstein's framework: a single capitalist world-economy divided into core, semi-periphery, and periphery |
| **Prebisch-Singer thesis** | Terms of trade for primary commodity exporters decline over time relative to manufactures |
| **GATT** | General Agreement on Tariffs and Trade — provisional, weak dispute enforcement |
| **WTO** | World Trade Organization — binding dispute resolution, broader mandate than GATT |
| **OLI Paradigm** | Ownership, Location, Internalization — framework for why firms become multinational |
| **Infant industry argument** | Temporary protection for new industries until they achieve scale (List, US/Germany 19th c.) |
| **Mercantilism** | State-driven trade policy maximizing exports and minimizing imports to accumulate national wealth |
| **Dutch Disease** | Commodity boom causes currency appreciation, harming non-resource tradable sectors |
| **Race to the Bottom** | Competitive deregulation of labor/environmental standards to attract FDI |
| **Enclave Economy** | Export sector with few backward linkages to the rest of the domestic economy |
| **Unequal Exchange** | Net transfer of surplus from periphery to core through unequal terms of trade |
