## 1. Current Situation

| Indicator | Data |
|-----------|------|
| Population | 160M+ (72% rural) |
| Family dairy farms | **1.4 million** (~7M people) |
| Typical farmer | 2 cows, income **Tk 31–60/day** |
| Milk consumption | **42 ml/person/day** vs FAO recommendation **280g/day** |
| Current production | 2.7M tonnes |
| Potential demand | 7.2M tonnes |
| **Chronic undernutrition** | **48% of children** |
| **Import share** | **27% of milk consumption** |

---

## 2. Bangladesh's Comparative Advantage

| Metric | Bangladesh | EU |
|--------|-----------|-----|
| Cost of production | **50% cheaper** (2-cow farm) | German farm (31 cows) |
| Jobs per million kg milk | **350 jobs** | **7.6 jobs** |
| Employment intensity | **46× more** | — |

> Bangladesh dairy is naturally competitive — it's cheaper AND creates more jobs. The problem is **unfair competition from subsidized imports**.

---

## 3. The Import Problem

### Scale
- 2007–08: imported **41,000 tonnes** at cost of **Tk 10.5 billion**
- 20–50% of skimmed milk powder from EU

### Price Crash (2009)
| Period | Global Milk Powder Price |
|--------|------------------------|
| Before crash | $4,500/tonne |
| After crash | **$2,000/tonne** |
| Local milk price fell | Tk 33 → **Tk 26/litre** |
| Farmer income loss | **Up to 40%** |

**Result:** Hundreds of farmers poured milk onto highways in protest.

---

## 4. Arla Foods — The EU Giant

| Fact | Detail |
|------|--------|
| Company | Danish-Swedish dairy cooperative |
| Bangladesh share | **>20% of national milk sales** (Dano brand) |
| EU subsidies received | **Nearly €1 billion since 2000** |
| 2009: exported to BD | 1.5M kg at **15% subsidized price** |
| Milk powder export earnings | **€112 million (2010)** |
| CSR project | Serves only **235 slum children** at 0.08% of net profits |

### The Subsidy Game
- EU claimed post-2005 subsidies no longer distort trade (placed in WTO "Green Box")
- Reality: Studies show decoupled payments **increase production** and **lower world prices**
- **31% of Danish dairy income from subsidies** (2006); **77% in Finland**
- German study: farmers earned only 49–59% of costs from milk sales (2004–08)

---

## 5. Policy Failure

| Policy | Details |
|--------|---------|
| Import tariffs | Fell from **75% (2007) → proposed 5%** |
| Subsidies to farmers | **Eliminated in 1996** |
| Budget allocation to dairy | **0.27%** — vs 2.7% GDP contribution |
| Only support | Milk Vita (monopoly processor) — low-interest loans |

---

## 6. Milk Vita Collapse

- Once procured **15 million litres** of milk
- After EU import competition: **<3 million litres**
- Illustrates how subsidized imports destroy domestic capacity

---

## 7. Policy Recommendations (Exam Answer)

For Q5b, recommend:

| Policy | Rationale |
|--------|-----------|
| **Protect dairy tariffs** | Reverse tariff cuts; maintain at protective levels |
| **Restore farmer subsidies** | Direct support to smallholders (2-cow farms) |
| **Invest in cold chain infrastructure** | Reduce spoilage, improve market access |
| **Enforce anti-dumping measures** | Counter EU subsidized exports |
| **Support Milk Vita revival** | Public procurement strengthens farmer confidence |
