## What Is This Topic About?

Why the global food crisis of 2007–08 happened — and Patnaik's argument that it was NOT caused by "too many people" or "rising demand in China/India," but by structural inequalities in how food is produced and consumed.

---

## Key Definitions

| Term | Simple Explanation |
|------|--------------------|
| **Food crisis** | When food prices spike and people go hungry |
| **Structural contradiction** | A fundamental conflict built into the system (not a temporary problem) |
| **Food vs feed** | Grain used to feed animals vs feed people directly — animals are inefficient |
| **Biofuels** | Fuel made from crops (corn ethanol) — competes with food production |
| **Poverty line manipulation** | Lowering the poverty standard to make it seem like fewer people are poor |
| **Terms of trade** | The ratio of export prices to import prices — gets worse for poor countries |

---

## Patnaik's 3 Contradictions — Simple Version

| Contradiction | Simple Explanation |
|---------------|-------------------|
| **1. Food vs Feed** | Rich people eat meat → animals eat grain → less grain for poor people. 7kg of grain → 1kg of beef — 95% of calories lost |
| **2. Food vs Export** | Land used to grow flowers/vegetables for export instead of food for local people |
| **3. Food vs Fuel** | US turns ⅓ of its corn into ethanol for cars instead of food for people |

---

## The Meat Problem — Simple Numbers

```
1 kg beef needs 7+ kg of grain

Grain (direct):   24,000 calories   ← feeds many people
       ↓
Cow (conversion):  only 1,100 calories left ← feeds one person
       ↓
Loss:  95% of calories wasted
```

**The more meat rich people eat, the less grain is available for poor people.**

---

## Who Eats What?

| Group | Grain per person/year | How they eat it |
|-------|----------------------|-----------------|
| **Rich countries (US/EU)** | ~900 kg | Mostly through meat, milk, eggs |
| **Poor countries** | ~130 kg | Directly as rice, wheat, maize |

> Rich countries consume **7 times more grain per person** than poor countries.

---

## Simple Analogy

> Imagine a village with limited grain. Rich families feed most of their grain to cows for beef. Poor families eat the grain directly as porridge. A global expert says "there's not enough grain because there are too many people!" Patnaik says: no — there's not enough because rich families are feeding it to cows.
