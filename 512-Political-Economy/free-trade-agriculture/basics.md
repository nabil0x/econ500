## What Is This Topic About?

The rules of international agricultural trade, how they were supposed to help developing countries, and why they actually hurt them — with a focus on the gap between rich countries' promises and their actual behavior.

---

## Key Definitions

| Term | Simple Explanation |
|------|--------------------|
| **Comparative advantage** | The theory that countries should specialize in what they produce most efficiently |
| **Dumping** | Selling exports below the cost of production (often with government subsidies) |
| **Subsidy** | Government money given to producers to lower their costs |
| **WTO Agreement on Agriculture (AoA)** | 1995 agreement that set the rules for global agricultural trade |
| **Market access** | How easy it is to sell products in another country |
| **Export subsidies** | Government payments that make exports cheaper on world markets |
| **Food sovereignty** | The right of countries to control their own food and agricultural policies |

---

## The Promise vs Reality — Simple Version

| What Developing Countries Were Promised | What Actually Happened |
|----------------------------------------|----------------------|
| "We'll cut our subsidies" | EU & US kept massive subsidies |
| "You'll get better prices for your crops" | Prices fell 53% |
| "Your farmers will benefit from new markets" | Cheap imports destroyed local farming |
| "Food security will improve" | Import dependency increased |

---

## How Dumping Destroys Local Farming

```
Rich country:                     Developing country:
  EU subsidizes farmers              Local farmer produces milk
  → milk sold cheaply on             → cannot compete with EU prices
    world market                     → sells cows, leaves farming
  → dairy farmers in the EU         → country imports even more milk
    survive on subsidies              → dependency deepens
                                    → farmers lose livelihoods
```

## Simple Analogy

> A small farmer sells mangoes for $1 each. A giant corporation starts selling mangoes for $0.30 each (because the government pays them $0.50 subsidy per mango). The small farmer goes out of business. Then the corporation raises prices to $2. That's dumping.
