## 1. Definition

**Washington Consensus** = coordinated campaign for global diffusion of market-oriented policies, organized through IFIs (IMF, World Bank). Term coined by **John Williamson (1990)**.

"Washington" = political Washington (Congress, administration) + technocratic Washington (IMF, World Bank, U.S. Treasury, Fed, think tanks).

## 3. Historical Context

| Period | Event |
|--------|-------|
| Pre-1980s | Import-substituting industrialization, state intervention |
| 1970s | Oil price spikes → developing countries borrowed heavily |
| 1979 | Volcker raised U.S. interest rates → debts unsustainable |
| 1982 | Mexico debt crisis → Latin American crisis |
| 1985 | Baker Plan — aid conditional on market reforms |
| 1990s | End of Cold War → unipolar U.S. dominance |

## 4. Mechanisms

| Mechanism | How It Works |
|-----------|-------------|
| **Conditionality** | Loans exchanged for adopting specific policies |
| **Structural Adjustment** | Comprehensive reform programs attached to WB loans |
| **"Shock therapy"** | Push reforms past point of no return |
| **Ex ante conditionality** | Withholding funds until policies adopted |
| **Technocrat networks** | U.S.-educated economists in developing countries enforced reforms |

### Key Promoters

| Actor | Role |
|-------|------|
| U.S. Government | Leading engineer; veto power in IFIs |
| IMF | Expanded mandate → market liberalization via conditionality |
| World Bank | Structural adjustment loans (~30% of disbursements by late 1980s) |
| Bilateral donors | Required IMF/WB compliance |

## 5. Babb & Kentikelenis's Critique

### A. Not Just Ideas — Power & Coercion
- Driven by organizational mechanisms (conditionality), U.S. hegemony, Wall Street interests

### B. Uneven Implementation
| Region | Treatment |
|--------|-----------|
| Latin America | "Star pupils" — most reforms |
| U.S. allies (Middle East) | More lenient |
| Sub-Saharan Africa | Non-compliance → stronger enforcement |
| China, Vietnam, India | Notably abstained |

### C. Devastating Outcomes
- Countries that most adopted reforms **failed to thrive**
- Increased unemployment and inequality
- China — which **ignored** WC — achieved astounding growth

### D. Erosion of Legitimacy
- By 2006, Dani Rodrik: *"Nobody really believes in the Washington Consensus anymore"*
- Stiglitz fired from World Bank for criticizing IMF
- Krugman, Sachs recanted

### E. The "Undercover" Consensus
- Consensus didn't die — went **undercover**
- IMF still promotes structural reforms in crisis lending (Eurozone bailouts)
- World Bank uses Ease of Doing Business rankings
- Reforms already enacted are **difficult to reverse**

## 6. Impacts on Developing Countries

| Area | Impact |
|------|--------|
| **Trade** | Tariffs collapsed (Latin America: 42% → <10%) |
| **Privatization** | Thousands of SOEs sold (often to foreign investors) |
| **Fiscal** | Austerity; cuts to subsidies and public services |
| **Labour** | Deregulation; weakened protections |
| **Inequality** | Increased in most reforming countries |
| **Policy space** | Dramatically narrowed |

## 7. Exam Note: Distinguish From Neoliberalism

| Washington Consensus | Neoliberalism |
|--------------------|---------------|
| Specific policy program (10 items) | Vague ideological label |
| Identifiable agents, processes, timelines | Broad philosophical concept |
| Williamson himself was more moderate than actual implementation | Often used pejoratively |
