## 🎯 Relevance

Provides the theoretical framework for understanding export-led growth vs food self-sufficiency debate. Directly applicable to any question about trade policy for developing countries.

---

## 1. Central Thesis

> The conflict between cash crops (export) and food crops (self-reliance) is fundamentally a conflict between **dependency** and **autonomy**.

### Two Competing Views

| View | Position |
|------|----------|
| **Left** | Cash crops = naked capitalism continuing colonial exploitation of peasants |
| **Right** | Cash crops = only way Africa can earn foreign exchange for health, education, nutrition |

---

## 2. Colonial Origins

| Period | Situation |
|--------|-----------|
| Pre-mercantilist Africa | **Food self-sufficient** |
| Colonial era | Cash crop plantations imposed to serve European needs |
| Colonial mechanisms | **Force and taxation** to extort land and labor |

### Example: Peanut Exploitation
- Peanuts bought from West African peasants in 1930s
- Sold in Britain at **7× what peasants received**

### Post-Colonial Continuity
- Post-independence governments often **outdid colonial administrations**
- **Mali example:** French required 5–10 kg cotton per adult (1929); by 1970, Malian government forced **48 kg per adult**
- Cash crops get better land, credit, research, infrastructure

## 3. Deteriorating Terms of Trade

| Year | Bananas for 1 Tractor |
|------|----------------------|
| 1960 | 3 tons |
| 1970 | **11 tons** |

| Year | 25 tons Rubber = |
|------|-----------------|
| 1960 | **6 tractors** |
| 1975 | **2 tractors** |

> *"Total loss of foreign exchange earnings to Africa due to falling prices... exceeded all foreign funds invested, loaned or granted to Africa"* (Lappé 1977)

---

## 4. Who Benefits from Cash Crop Dependency?

| Group | Interest |
|-------|----------|
| **Comprador bourgeoisie** | Import-export business, dependent on world market |
| **State bureaucracy** | Owns plantations, works with international lending agencies |

> World Bank & IMF conditionality shape African economic policies.

---

## 5. The Dependency-Autonomy Framework

```
Cash Crop Economy
       │
       ├── "Driving belt" between African economies and developed countries
       ├── Mechanism: unequal exchange → wealth extraction
       ├── Eroded subsistence economy (drained labor, introduced imports)
       └── Peasants in debt trap: produce to repay loans for seeds, fertilizer
```

---

## 6. Eight Policy Conclusions (Darkoh & Ould-Mey)

| # | Recommendation |
|---|---------------|
| 1 | Reject export-led growth model → food self-sufficiency |
| 2 | Empower peasants (60–80% of agricultural labor = women) |
| 3 | Increase trade between African states |
| 4 | Adopt coherent national food security policies |
| 5 | Collective self-reliance (sub-regional food security) |
| 6 | Science & technology for agriculture |
| 7 | Training and technology access for women farmers |
| 8 | Political will to implement policies |
