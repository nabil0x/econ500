| Claim | Reality |
|-------|---------|
| Each country specializes in what it does most efficiently | But agriculture dominated by 3–5 firms controlling 40%+ of global trade |
| Total output increases, prices fall | Developing countries' farmers can't compete with subsidized Western producers |
| Displaced farmers find work in cities | Dumping destroys livelihoods faster than other sectors can absorb |
| World Bank predicted $250B gains | Revised to $40–60B after signing |

## 2. Promise vs Reality

| Promise | Reality |
|---------|---------|
| Developed countries would reduce subsidies | U.S. limits set at already-reached levels |
| Higher world commodity prices for farmers | FAO index fell **53% in real terms** (1997–2003) |
| Food security through trade | Import bills rose **40% for LDCs** (1995–96) |

## 3. Arguments Against Free Trade in Agriculture

### Not a Level Playing Field
- 3–5 firms control 40%+ of many commodity markets (Cargill, Nestlé)
- Nestlé profit ($9.7B, 2007) > GDP of 65 poorest countries
- Only **10–15% of food traded internationally**, yet AoA pressures ALL agriculture to be trade-oriented

### Dumping
| Commodity | Dumping Margin |
|-----------|---------------|
| Cotton | **57%** |
| Wheat | **40%** |
| Corn | 25–30% |
| Rice | **20%** |

> Effect: Drives developing country farmers out of local markets

### Selective Liberalization
| Protected (Rich Countries) | Liberalized |
|---------------------------|-------------|
| Sugar (where South is competitive) | Tea, coffee, cocoa, bananas (don't produce) |
| Cotton | Tropical commodities |

> Developing countries gained **little new market access**

### Food Security Impact
- Public food stocks dismantled under IMF/WB pressure
- Food import bills for LDCs expected **4x higher in 2008 vs 2000**
- Vicious cycle: imports → depressed local prices → less domestic production → more imports

---

## 4. WTO Agreement on Agriculture (AoA) — 3 Pillars

| Pillar | Purpose |
|--------|---------|
| **Market Access** | Replace non-tariff barriers with tariffs, then reduce |
| **Export Subsidies** | Discipline below-cost export payments |
| **Domestic Subsidies** | Categorize and cap internal support to farmers |

---

## 5. Alternatives Proposed

| Alternative | Description |
|-------------|-------------|
| **Food Sovereignty** | Countries must control their own food policies |
| **Agroecology** | Cuba example: 80% input loss → urban gardens fed 50% of Havana |
| **Fair Trade** | Cadbury switching Dairy Milk to Fairtrade tripled Ghana cocoa exports |
| **Slow Food movement** | 100,000+ members in 132 countries |
| **Nayakrishi Andolon (Bangladesh)** | 170,000 farm households practicing pesticide-free ecological agriculture |
