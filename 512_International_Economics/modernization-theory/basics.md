# Basics — Modernization Theory

> Foundational concepts before diving into the detailed arguments.

---

## What Is This Topic About?

A theory from the 1950s–60s that argued all countries develop through the same stages, and poor countries just need to follow the path Western countries took. It was hugely influential — and hugely criticized.

---

## Key Definitions

| Term | Simple Explanation |
|------|--------------------|
| **Modernization** | The process of becoming "modern" — industrial, urban, technologically advanced |
| **Traditional society** | In MT: a society based on subsistence agriculture, kinship, and custom (vs modern rationality) |
| **Eurocentrism** | Viewing the world from a European/Western perspective, assuming it's the norm |
| **Linear development** | The idea that development follows a straight line (stage 1 → 2 → 3 → ...) |
| **Take-off** | Rostow's term for the moment when industrialization becomes self-sustaining |

---

## Rostow's 5 Stages — Simple Version

```
Stage 1: Traditional Society
    │  (farming, barter, no modern tech)
    ▼
Stage 2: Pre-conditions for Take-off
    │  (roads, ports, savings, new ideas)
    ▼
Stage 3: Take-off
    │  (factories, machines, growth takes off)
    ▼
Stage 4: Drive to Maturity
    │  (diverse industries, less dependence on imports)
    ▼
Stage 5: Age of Mass Consumption
    (cars, TVs, services, high living standards)
```

**The assumption:** Every country MUST pass through these stages in this order.

---

## Why Modernization Theory Emerged

| Factor | Simple Explanation |
|--------|-------------------|
| **Post-WWII** | Western countries wanted to help (and influence) poor countries |
| **Cold War** | Needed to win hearts and minds against communism |
| **Marshall Plan success** | Rebuilding Europe worked — why not apply same model globally? |
| **Colonialism's end** | Needed a new language (not "civilizing" but "developing") |

---

## The Core Assumption (That Got Criticized)

> "Developing countries are where Europe was 200 years ago. If they copy us, they will become like us."

**The problem with this assumption:**
- Europe developed BY exploiting colonies — poor countries can't do that
- Every country has unique history, culture, circumstances
- "Traditional" doesn't mean "inferior" — it means different

---

## Simple Analogy

> Modernization theory is like saying every person must go through the same life stages: baby → child → teenager → adult. A 40-year-old who never went to college is "stuck" at an earlier stage. But this ignores that people have different paths, different starting points, and different definitions of success.
