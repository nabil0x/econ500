## What Is This Topic About?

How EU-subsidized milk powder imports destroyed Bangladesh's domestic dairy industry — the clearest example of how "free trade" in practice hurts poor farmers. Also your best case study for exam answers.

---

## Key Definitions

| Term | Simple Explanation |
|------|--------------------|
| **Subsidy dumping** | Rich countries pay their farmers to produce → farmers sell cheaply on world markets → destroys local producers in poor countries |
| **Decoupled payments** | Subsidies that EU claimed don't distort trade (put in WTO "green box") — but actually still increase production and lower prices |
| **Import substitution** | When cheap imports replace local production — processors switch from fresh milk to cheaper imported powder |
| **Dairy farmer livelihoods** | For Bangladesh's 1.4M small dairy farms (2 cows each), this is their entire income — they have no safety net |

## The Basic Problem

```
EU DAIRY SYSTEM
EU gives €5 billion/year in dairy subsidies
        │
        ▼
Arla Foods (Danish dairy giant) produces cheap milk powder
        │
        ▼
Exports to Bangladesh at subsidized prices
        │
        ▼
BANGLADESH DAIRY SYSTEM
Local farmers produce fresh milk at Tk 33/litre
        │
        ▼
Processors buy imported powder instead (cheaper)
        │
        ▼
Local milk price falls → farmers lose income
        │
        ▼
Farmers pour milk on highways (2009 protests)
```

## The Insane Numbers

| Metric | Bangladesh | EU |
|--------|-----------|-----|
| Jobs per million kg milk | **350 jobs** | 7.6 jobs |
| Cost of production | **50% cheaper** | More expensive |
| Subsidies received | **None** | €1 billion (Arla alone) |

> Bangladesh dairy is naturally MORE competitive than the EU — it's cheaper AND creates 46x more jobs. The problem is subsidies, not efficiency.

## Simple Analogy

> Two milk sellers in a village. One is local — sells fresh milk at a fair price. Another is backed by a rich relative who gives them free money. The backed seller can sell milk below cost. The local seller goes out of business. Then the backed seller raises prices. That's what EU-Arla did to Bangladesh dairy farmers.

## The Policy Failure

| What Bangladesh Did Wrong | What Should Have Been Done |
|--------------------------|---------------------------|
| Cut dairy tariffs from 75% → proposed 5% | Kept protective tariffs |
| Eliminated farmer subsidies (1996) | Supported small farmers |
| Spent only 0.27% of budget on dairy (vs 2.7% GDP) | Invested proportionally |
| Let Milk Vita collapse (15M litres → <3M) | Protected procurement system |
