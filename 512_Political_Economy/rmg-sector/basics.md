## What Is This Topic About?

Bangladesh's biggest industry — Ready-Made Garments (RMG) — and why its success story has worrying cracks. Imports are growing faster than exports, value addition is falling, and the sector may be becoming more of a "cut-and-trade" operation than actual manufacturing.

---

## Key Definitions

| Term | Simple Explanation |
|------|--------------------|
| **RMG (Ready-Made Garments)** | The garment manufacturing industry — Bangladesh's largest export sector |
| **Value addition** | How much value a country adds to a product. If you import fabric at $10 and export a shirt at $12, value addition = $2 (only 17%) |
| **Backward linkage** | The domestic industries that supply inputs to RMG (fabric, yarn, accessories) |
| **Forward linkage** | What happens after RMG production (logistics, retail, branding) |
| **Over-invoicing** | Inflating import prices on paper to move money out of the country illegally |
| **LDC graduation** | Bangladesh will lose Least Developed Country status in 2026 → loses trade preferences |

---

## The Core Problem — Value Addition is Dying

```
2010s: Raw materials = 69% of export value  →  30% value added in Bangladesh
Today:  Raw materials = 83-90% of export value →  Only 10-17% value added

Bangladesh is importing more and more fabric (instead of making it)
and getting less and less of the final product's value.
```

**The sector is becoming:** Import fabric → cut & sew → export. That's NOT real manufacturing.

---

## Import vs Export — The Divergence

| Period | Import Growth | Export Growth | Gap |
|--------|--------------|---------------|-----|
| FY22 | **+51.2%** | +24.6% | 2x |

> Imports growing twice as fast as exports = value addition shrinking.

---

## Why This Matters

| If This Continues | Consequence |
|-------------------|-------------|
| Thin profit margins | Exporters can't survive |
| Capital flight | Money leaves the country disguised as import payments |
| LDC graduation | Without GSP, exports become less competitive |
| Competitors (Vietnam) | Have better FTAs, lower tariffs on inputs |

---

## Simple Analogy

> A bakery buys flour for $8, bakes bread, and sells it for $10. Value added = $2 (20%). Over time, flour prices rise to $9.20, but customers won't pay more. Value added = $0.80 (8%). The bakery is barely surviving. That's Bangladesh's RMG sector.

---

## The UNCTAD Argument (Bigger Picture)

UNCTAD says developing countries should shift from **export-led growth** (relying on selling to rich countries) to **domestic-demand-led growth** (building local markets and consumers).

For Bangladesh RMG: instead of just exporting cheap clothes, develop the domestic market, build backward linkages (textile mills, fabric production), and move up the value chain.
