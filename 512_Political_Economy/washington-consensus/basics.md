## What Is This Topic About?

A set of 10 economic policies that the IMF, World Bank, and U.S. Treasury pushed on developing countries from the 1980s onward. It's the most concrete example of how rich countries and international institutions shape developing countries' economic policies.

---

## Key Definitions

| Term | Simple Explanation |
|------|--------------------|
| **Washington Consensus** | 10 policy prescriptions for developing countries (named by John Williamson, 1990) |
| **"Washington"** | Not the city — means IMF, World Bank, U.S. Treasury, Federal Reserve |
| **Conditionality** | "If you want our loan, you must adopt our policies" |
| **Structural Adjustment** | A loan that comes with conditions requiring major economic reforms |
| **Shock therapy** | Implementing all reforms at once, very quickly |
| **Fiscal discipline** | Keeping government spending low (austerity) |
| **Trade liberalization** | Opening markets by removing import restrictions |
| **Privatization** | Selling state-owned companies to private owners |

## The 10 Policies — The "Big 3" for Exams

| # | Policy | Simple Meaning | Key Critique |
|---|--------|---------------|--------------|
| **1** | **Fiscal discipline** | Cut budget deficits | Austerity hurts poor people |
| **6** | **Trade liberalization** | Remove import barriers | Local producers can't compete |
| **8** | **Privatization** | Sell state enterprises | Jobs lost, foreign control |

> **Exam tip:** Memorize these 3 — they're the most frequently asked about.

## How It Worked — The Mechanism

```
Developing country needs money (debt crisis)
                │
                ▼
Goes to IMF/World Bank for loan
                │
                ▼
IMF says: "We'll lend you money IF you:"
    1. Cut government spending
    2. Remove import restrictions
    3. Sell state companies
    4. Deregulate your economy
    5. Open to foreign investment
                │
                ▼
Country adopts policies (conditionality)
```

---

## Simple Analogy

> Imagine you're drowning in debt. A bank says: "I'll give you a loan — but you must sell your car, quit your job, and let me decide how you spend your money." You might have no choice but to accept. That's conditionality.
