> **Instructions:** Answer any five questions. All parts of a question must be answered consecutively.

### Question 1 [3+5+6=14]
A: What is the main concern of Utilitarianism?

B: Critically explain the concept of preference-satisfaction utilitarianism as a basis for modern welfare economics.

C: What are Utilitarianism's weaknesses? How does Rawls' 'Theory of Justice' challenge classical utilitarianism? Explain.

### Question 2 [6+8=14]

B: Discuss the policy implications of the EKC, including the potential for decoupling economic growth from environmental degradation.

### Question 3 [6+4+4=14]
A: Distinguish between flow and stock pollutants. Analyze efficient levels of flow pollutants and stock pollutants.

B: Explain the role of spatial differentiation for emission targets.

C: Find the efficient solution ($A_M^*$ and $A^*$) for uniformly mixed and short-lived stock pollutant with the following information:

    Total Benefit, $B = 9M - 0.2M^2$
    Total Damage, $D = 0.2A^2$ for $A > 0$

    and in steady state assume that $A = 2M$

    where $M$ is the Emissions flow and $A$ is the Stock Pollutant.

### Question 5 [6+5+3=14]
A: Define compensating variation (CV) and equivalent variation (EV) in consumer preference. Show the relation among CV, EV and Marshallian Consumer Surplus (MCS).

B: Suppose that an individual has the utility function $U = E^\alpha + Y^\beta$ where $E$ is some index of environmental quality and $Y$ is income. From an initial situation where $E = 1$ and $Y = 1$, calculate Compensating Surplus (CS) and Equivalent Surplus (ES) for an increase in $E$ to the level 2 and for a decrease to the level 0.5.

C: What is the difference between direct and indirect methods of environmental valuation?
