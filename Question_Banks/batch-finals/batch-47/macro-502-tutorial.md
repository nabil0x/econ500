## Tutorial 2

> **Instructions:** Answer any **one** question.

### Question 1 — Ramsey-Cass-Koopmans [5+7+4+4]

Consider a Ramsey-Cass-Koopmans model with the production function $Y = F(K, AL)$ and real interest rate $r$; household's utility function takes the form

$$U = \int_0^\infty e^{-\rho t} u(c(t)) dt$$

and there is CRRA (constant-relative-risk-aversion) utility

$$u(c(t)) = \frac{c(t)^{1-\theta}}{1-\theta}.$$

**A:** Construct Household budget constraint and show that there is no-Ponzi-game condition. [5]

**B:** Solve Euler equation for households' maximization problem. [7]

**C:** Describe the behavior of the economy in terms of the evolution of $c$ and $k$. [4]

**D:** How does the competitive equilibrium maximize the welfare of the representative household? [4]

### Question 2 — Overlapping Generations (OLG) [5+4+3+4+4]

Suppose $Y_t = F(K_t, A_t L_t)$, with having constant returns to scale and the intensive form of the production function satisfying the Inada conditions. Suppose also that $A_{t+1} = (1+g)A_t$, $L_{t+1} = (1+n)L_t$ and the production function is Cobb-Douglas. Assume time is discrete rather than continuous and each individual lives for only two periods.

**A:** Solve the individual's maximization problem where $\theta$ determines how much individuals vary in response to differences between $r$ and $\rho$. [5]

**B:** Find an expression for $k_{t+1}$ as a function of $k_t$. [4]

**C:** Describe how each of the following affects $k_{t+1}$ as a function of $k_t$:
    (i) A rise in $n$.
    (ii) A rise in $\alpha$. [3]

**D:** Sketch $k_{t+1}$ as a function of $k_t$. Does the economy have a balanced growth path? If the initial level of $k$ differs from the value on the balanced growth path, does the economy converge to the balanced growth path? [4]

**E:** Find the rate of convergence of $k$ to $k^*$. [4]

### Question 3 — R&D (Romer/GH/AH) [2+6+6+6]

Consider a simplified version of the models of R&D and growth developed by P. Romer (1990), Gross and Helpman (1991) and Aghion and Howitt (1992) where $\theta$ reflects the effect of the existing stock of knowledge on the success of R&D.

**A:** Explain the simplification process to construct the framework. [2]

**B:** Critically explain the role of $a_L$ (the labor force used in the R&D sector) for the dynamics of knowledge accumulation without considering capital into the model. [6]

**C:** Assume that $\theta + \beta < 1$ and $n > 0$, and that the economy is on its balanced growth path. Describe how each of the following changes affects the $g_A = 0$ and $g_K = 0$ lines and the position of the economy in $(g_A, g_K)$ space at the moment of the change:
    (i) An increase in $n$.
    (ii) An increase in $a_L$.
    (iii) An increase in $\beta$. [6]

**D:** Assume that $\theta + \beta = 1$ and $n = 0$.
    (i) Find the value that $A/K$ must have for $g_A$ and $g_K$ to be equal.
    (ii) Find the growth rate of $A$ and $K$ when $g_A = g_K$.
    (iii) How does an increase in $s$ affect the long-run growth rate of the economy? [6]

### Question 1 — R&D with Learning by Doing [4+4+4+4+4]

Suppose firm $i$'s output is $Y_i(t) = K_i(t)^\alpha [A(t)L_i(t)]^{1-\alpha}$ and $A(t) = BK(t)$. Here $K_i$ and $L_i$ are the amounts of capital and labor used by firm $i$, and $K$ is the aggregate capital stock. Capital and labor earn their private marginal products. The economy is populated by infinitely lived households that own the economy's capital stock. Individual's discount rate is $\rho$ and their instantaneous utility function is logarithmic. The utility of the representative household takes the constant-relative-risk aversion form and $\theta$ is the coefficient of the relative risk aversion. Population growth is zero.

**A:** How is the long-run growth endogenous which is affected by the saving rate according to learning by doing? [4]

**B:** When output is produced by competitive firms, what will be the demand for the holder of the patent on an idea and the profit? [4]

**C:** Describe the assumptions to construct the model. [4]

**D:** When will the R&D be zero and how will the long-run growth be determined? [4]

**E:** What will be the socially optimal level of $L_a$? Explain how it is different from the equilibrium level of $L_a$. [4]

### Question 2 — Real Business Cycle [3+3+4+6+4]

Consider the infinitely lived representative household that derives utility from both consumption ($c_t$) and leisure ($1-l_t$). The production function of the representative firm is given by

$$f(k_t, l_t, z_t) = z_t k_t^\alpha l_t^{1-\alpha}$$

$$z_{t+1} = G z_t^\rho \varepsilon_t$$

where $z_t$ is the productivity parameter, $\varepsilon_t$ is a white noise error term.

**A:** Construct the household's problem and using log utility derive the short-run labor supply curve. Explain the relationship among leisure, real wage, interest rate. [3]

**B:** Prove that long run labor supply is independent of wage when $\varphi = \sigma$. [3]

**C:** Assuming $\sigma = 1$ and functional form of utility of leisure is $v(h) = (1-h)^{1-\theta}/(1-\theta)$, find the elasticity of labor supply. [4]

**D:** Mention two important limitations of previous business cycle models. Discuss Hansen's indivisible labor supply solution as an attempt to overcome these limitations. [6]

**E:** What are the stylized facts of real business cycle? [4]
