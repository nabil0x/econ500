## Slide 2

Population growth

Population growth is the net change in population within a specific period in a given area.

Population growth can be measured in a neighbourhood, country, or even global level!

Population growth can be positive, can well be negative! It depends on the nett change in population over a specific time frame.

The three leading causes of population growth are births, deaths, and migration. Births and deaths are seen as natural causes of population change.

Components of population growth

Natural increase/decrease: Birth rate, death rate, infant mortality rate, life expectancy

Net migration: Immigration, emigration, net migration rate

Childbearing patterns: Total fertility rate, replacement level fertility

Patterns related to death: Crude death rate, age-specific death rate

Age structure (distribution of age groups): Dependency ratio, median age

## Slide 3

Population growth and economic growth

Population growth and economic growth are closely related to one another. For example, productivity is an important factor in economic growth.

How might productivity be important to population growth?

A greater population means that there is a larger workforce. A larger workforce means that there is potential for higher productivity to produce more goods — this results in greater output (GDP)! Not only is there a greater supply of workers, but there is also a greater demand for goods and services as well. Greater demand and supply will lead to an increase in overall economic growth.

More people in a country will also result in higher tax revenues for the government. The government can use the increased tax revenue to build infrastructure or improve welfare programs. Lastly, a higher population increases the probability of innovation in the free market.

In brief, positive economic effects are: more people means more output, more tax revenue and innovation in market.

## Slide 4

Population growth and economic growth: The darker side

Greater population growth may exacerbate the problem of resource scarcity.

If a country is barely providing resources to its current population, people will not be able to access resources since there will be too many people demanding too few resources.

Population growth can also put pressure on certain areas where people migrate to, such as cities. Cities tend to have more people living in them as opposed to rural areas; as such, cities can become overburdened with too many people living in them.

Traffic congestion and pollution are often problems in these areas.

Traffic congestion erodes productive time ……..results in fuel inefficiency….. Contributes to air pollution, thereby increasing externality.

Population growth not only can adversely affect economic growth, can also contribute to environmental degradation.

The rise in the number of megacities is the most visible evidence of the accelerating global trend towards urbanization.
