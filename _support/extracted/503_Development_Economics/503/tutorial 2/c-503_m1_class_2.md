## Slide 10

Components of population growth  (revisiting)

Natural increase/decrease: Birth rate, death rate, infant mortality rate, life expectancy

Net migration: Immigration, emigration, net migration rate

Childbearing patterns: Total fertility rate, replacement level fertility

Patterns related to death: Crude death rate, age-specific death rate

Age structure (distribution of age groups): Dependency ratio, median age

Definitions

Birth rate: Birth rate, also known as natality, is the total number of live human births per 1,000 population for a given period divided by the length of the period in years.

Death rate: Death rate or mortality rate is a measure of the number of deaths (in general, or due to a specific cause) in a particular population, scaled to the size of that population, per unit of time.

Why the mortality rates in advanced economies are higher than that in South Asian countries?

## Slide 11

Reducing Death Rates: Fighting against diseases

Death rates have been declining across regions due to advancements in knowledge, technology, improvements in healthcare, etc.

That power has so far eradicated two infectious diseases: smallpox (1980) and rinderpest (2011). We are also getting closer to eradicating polio and Guinea worm disease.

But can we eradicate all infections from the world?

Louis Pasteur once said that “it is within the power of man to eradicate infection from the earth”.

Smallpox: From vaccine to eradication took 200 years!

Bangladesh’s population is projected to increase until the 2050s, when the peak will arrive. Then it will start to decline.
