## Tutorial 1 — Solow Growth Model

**Time:** 50 minutes | **Marks:** 20

Consider a closed economy with Cobb-Douglas production function:
> Y(t) = K(t)^α [A(t)L(t)]^(1-α), 0 < α < 1

where labor L(t) grows at rate n, technology A(t) grows at rate g, and capital depreciates at rate δ. A constant fraction s of output is saved.

### Question 1
a) Why is the Solow model referred to as a neoclassical growth model? Derive the dynamic equation of capital per effective worker and explain the conditions under which the economy converges to a steady state. (6)

b) Examine the transition process of capital per effective worker after a permanent decrease in the depreciation rate δ. How would a decline in population growth rate due to demographic transition affect Bangladesh's steady-state capital per worker? (4)

c) Use phase-diagram intuition to explain the adjustment path of capital per effective worker, output per effective worker, and consumption per effective worker. (4)

d) Suppose the economy initially operates at the Golden Rule level of capital. Examine how a permanent increase in the saving rate affects welfare over time. Is it possible for such a policy to improve long-run welfare? Justify analytically. (3)

e) Critically evaluate the Solow model's prediction of conditional convergence. Discuss at least two reasons why empirical growth experiences of developing countries may deviate from this prediction. (3)

## Tutorial 2 — Ramsey-Cass-Koopmans Model

**Time:** 50 minutes | **Marks:** 20

Consider a continuous-time Ramsey-Cass-Koopmans Model where aggregate output:
> Y(t) = F(K(t), A(t)L(t))

Technology A(t) grows exogenously, labor L(t) grows at rate n. Real interest rate is r(t). Representative household maximizes:

> U = ∫₀^∞ e^{-ρt} u(c(t)) dt

where instantaneous utility follows CRRA (Constant Relative Risk Aversion).

### Question 1
a) i) Describe the differential equations governing k and ċ. (4)
   ii) Use them to determine the k̇=0 and ċ=0 loci. (3)
   iii) Illustrate and interpret the phase diagram. (3)

b) Briefly explain the socially optimal level of capital and the Golden Rule level of capital. (2)

c) Estimate the speed of adjustment through a Taylor rule approximation. Determine the value of φ (the speed of adjustment) assuming values of independent variables. Why is this value different from the Solow model? (8)

---

### Question 2
d) Consider a closed economy and the government permanently raises government spending while financing it through lump-sum taxation. Explain how the phase diagram for capital and consumption changes. Analyze whether a temporary increase in government purchases can have different welfare implications compared to a permanent increase. (6)

## Key Concepts

| Concept | Definition |
|---------|------------|
| **Steady state** | k̇=0 where investment = break-even investment |
| **Golden Rule** | k^gold maximizes steady-state consumption: f'(k^gold) = n+g+δ |
| **Conditional convergence** | Poorer countries grow faster controlling for steady-state determinants |
| **Ramsey model** | Endogenous saving rate — households optimize consumption path |
| **CRRA utility** | u(c) = (c^(1-θ)-1)/(1-θ); constant intertemporal elasticity of substitution |
| **Phase diagram** | k̇ and ċ loci showing saddle-path stability |
| **Speed of adjustment** | Rate at which economy converges to steady state; faster in Solow than Ramsey |
| **Lump-sum tax** | Non-distortionary tax — doesn't affect incentives at margin |

## Tutorial 3 — Endogenous Growth & R&D

**Time:** 50 minutes | **Marks:** 20

**Source:** MainQ.pdf (Page 12) — Models by P. Romer (1990), Grossman & Helpman (1991), Aghion & Howitt (1992)

Consider a simplified version of the R&D-based growth model where θ reflects the effect of the existing stock of knowledge on the success of R&D and β is a shift parameter. Both the goods-producing sector and the R&D-producing sector use the full stock of knowledge A. The labor force used in the R&D sector is aᴸ.

### Question 1
a) Critically explain the role of aᴸ (the labor force used in the R&D sector) for the dynamics of knowledge accumulation, without considering capital in the model. Is there any long-run growth effect from a change in aᴸ? (6 marks)

b) Assume that θ + β < 1 and n > 0, and that the economy is on its balanced growth path. Describe how each of the following changes affects the ġᴬ = 0 and ġᴷ = 0 lines and the position of the economy in (gᴬ, gᴷ) space at the moment of the change:
   (i) An increase in n.
   (ii) A decrease in aᴷ (capital share in R&D). (6 marks)

c) Compare two cases: (i) β + θ < 1, and (ii) β + θ = 1 with n = 0. What is the difference in the dynamics of the growth rates of capital and knowledge between these two cases? (4 marks)

d) Briefly explain the nature of Knowledge and the determinants of the allocation of resources to R&D. (4 marks)

> **Note:** This is an advanced growth theory question bridging Solow (exogenous) and endogenous technical change. Romer shows that profit-driven R&D can generate sustained growth — unlike Solow where technology is a free good.
